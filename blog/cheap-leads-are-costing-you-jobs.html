Cheap Leads and Cheap Clicks Are Costing You Customers

"The leads were junk" is almost never about the leads. It is usually about what happened after the click.

In short

When a brand says the traffic or leads were junk, the cause is usually one of three things: the campaign optimised for cheap clicks or leads instead of buyers, the landing page or form let anyone through, or nobody followed up quickly. Fixing those gaps changes results without changing the traffic source.

Key facts
  • Optimising for cheap clicks or leads attracts the wrong people.
  • Forms and pages should filter for real intent.
  • Slow follow-up wastes good leads and shoppers.
  • Lead quality is usually a process problem, not a traffic problem.

It is one of the most common things we hear on a first call: "We tried ads, but the leads were junk," or "We got lots of clicks but no sales." Look closer and the traffic is rarely the problem. The system around it is.

Gap one: optimising for the wrong thing

If a campaign is told to find cheap clicks or cheap leads, the platform will find people who click and fill in forms cheaply, not people who buy. For D2C, campaigns should optimise for purchases and profitable return. For B2B, they should optimise for qualified leads and meetings, not raw form fills.

Gap two: no filter on intent

A one-tap instant form or a vague landing page lets anyone through. For B2B, a few qualifying questions (company size, role, timeline) remove most of the people who were never going to buy. For D2C, a clear offer and product page do the same job.

Gap three: nobody follows up

Good B2B leads go cold when nobody replies for a day. Good D2C shoppers leave when there is no abandoned cart or browse follow-up. The traffic was fine; the follow-up was missing.

Two campaigns on the same account can land at very different costs per customer. The difference usually isn’t the market; it’s which campaign reaches the right people, and what happens after they click.

What to measure instead

Judge campaigns on cost per customer, return on ad spend and cost per qualified meeting, not cost per click or cost per lead alone.

Common questions

Why are my leads low quality?

Usually because the campaign optimises for cheap leads, the form lets anyone through, or follow-up is slow.

What should D2C brands optimise for?

Purchases and profitable return on ad spend, not clicks.

What should B2B companies optimise for?

Qualified leads and booked meetings, not raw form fills.
