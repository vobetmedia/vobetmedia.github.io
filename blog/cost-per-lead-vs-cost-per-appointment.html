Cost Per Lead vs Cost Per Appointment vs Cost Per Customer

Three numbers, three different questions. Here is which one to watch and when.

In short

Cost per lead is ad spend divided by leads. Cost per appointment divides cost per lead by the share of leads that book. Cost per customer divides cost per appointment by the share of appointments that close (or, for ecommerce, ad spend divided by new customers). Cost per lead is the easiest to see but the least reliable on its own; cost per customer is what decides profit.

Key facts
  • Cost per lead = ad spend ÷ leads.
  • Cost per appointment = cost per lead ÷ booking rate.
  • Cost per customer = cost per appointment ÷ close rate.
  • Profit is decided by cost per customer, not cost per lead.

Ad platforms make cost per lead easy to see, so it is the number most people judge campaigns on. It is also the number most likely to mislead you on its own.

Cost per lead

Ad spend ÷ number of leads. Useful for comparing campaigns and creative. On our Western Roof Tiling account, for example, two campaigns ran at $21.44 and $37.54 per lead.

Cost per appointment

Cost per lead ÷ the share of leads that book. This brings in what happens after the form: speed of reply, qualification and follow-up. Two campaigns with the same cost per lead can have very different costs per appointment.

Cost per customer

Cost per appointment ÷ the share of appointments that close. For an ecommerce brand it is simpler: ad spend ÷ new customers. This is the number that decides whether marketing is profitable.

A worked example

Illustrative numbers only: $25 per lead, 40% of leads book, 30% of appointments close. Cost per appointment is $62.50 ($25 ÷ 0.4) and cost per customer is about $208 ($62.50 ÷ 0.3). A cheaper $15 lead where only 10% book and 20% close costs $750 per customer.

Which number to watch

  • Use cost per lead to compare ads and audiences week to week.
  • Use cost per appointment to judge follow-up and qualification.
  • Use cost per customer to decide budget.

The cheapest lead is often the most expensive customer. Read why cheap leads and cheap clicks cost you customers.

Common questions

What is the difference between cost per lead and cost per appointment?

Cost per lead counts every form fill; cost per appointment divides that by the share of leads that actually book.

How do I calculate cost per customer?

Divide cost per appointment by your close rate, or for ecommerce divide ad spend by new customers.

Which metric matters most?

Cost per customer, because it decides profit. Cost per lead is useful for comparing ads but misleading on its own.
