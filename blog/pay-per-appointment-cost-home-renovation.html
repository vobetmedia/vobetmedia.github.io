How Much Does Pay-Per-Appointment Cost for Home Renovation Companies?

The price per appointment is only half the answer. What matters is what each appointment turns into.

In short

There is no single market price for pay-per-appointment leads for home renovation companies: it depends on the project type, location and how the vendor defines an appointment. The useful number is your own: cost per appointment equals cost per lead divided by the share of leads that book. Compare that with what an appointment is worth to you, and check exclusivity, cancellation rules and who owns the ad account before signing.

Key facts
  • Cost per appointment = cost per lead ÷ booking rate.
  • Prices vary by project type, area and how "appointment" is defined.
  • Check exclusivity, no-show and cancellation terms.
  • Running ads in your own account keeps the data you pay for.

"How much does a booked appointment cost?" is one of the first questions renovation and remodeling companies ask. The honest answer is that it depends, but you can work out your own number in a few minutes.

How pay-per-appointment works

With pay-per-appointment, a vendor runs the marketing and only charges you when a homeowner books an estimate or consultation. It sounds risk-free. The details decide whether it is good value: what counts as an appointment, whether it is shared with other contractors, and what happens when a homeowner cancels or does not show.

Work out your own cost per appointment

If you run ads yourself (or with an agency in your own account), the maths is simple:

Cost per appointment = cost per lead ÷ the share of leads that book an appointment.

Example, not a quote: if you pay $30 per lead and one in three leads books, each appointment costs about $90 ($30 ÷ 0.33). If faster follow-up lifts that to one in two, the same leads now cost about $60 per appointment. Nothing changed in the ads; the follow-up did the work.

For real lead costs, our case studies show three client accounts: from $3.68 to $29.24 per lead depending on business type and campaign.

Then compare it with what an appointment is worth

An appointment is only worth what it turns into. Multiply your close rate by your average project value to see what a typical appointment is worth to you. If that number is well above your cost per appointment, you can afford to grow.

What to check before signing a pay-per-appointment deal

  • Exclusivity. Is the appointment sold only to you?
  • Definition. Is it a confirmed time with a homeowner, or just a phone number?
  • Cancellations and no-shows. Do you get credited?
  • Project fit. Can you filter by project type, budget and area?
  • Ownership. Who owns the ads, the data and the audience when you stop paying?

Renting appointments or owning the engine

Pay-per-appointment rents results: when payments stop, appointments stop. Running ads, forms and follow-up in your own accounts builds data and audiences you keep. Read why brands should own their growth engine.

Common questions

How much does pay-per-appointment cost for home renovation companies?

There is no single market price; it varies by project type, area and how the vendor defines an appointment. Work out your own number by dividing cost per lead by your booking rate.

How do I calculate cost per appointment?

Divide your cost per lead by the share of leads that book. For example, $30 per lead with one in three booking is about $90 per appointment.

Is pay-per-appointment better than pay-per-lead?

Not automatically. Compare exclusivity, cancellation terms and ownership, and what each appointment turns into for your business.

How can I lower my cost per appointment?

Reply to leads faster, qualify them with a few form questions, and follow up consistently so more leads book.
