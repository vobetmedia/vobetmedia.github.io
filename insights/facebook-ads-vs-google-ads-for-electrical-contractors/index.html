Facebook Ads vs Google Ads for Electrical Contractors

They are not rivals. They reach homeowners at different moments — and electrical work is varied enough to need both.

In short

For most electrical contractors, Google Ads capture people already searching for an electrician and suit urgent and ready-to-book jobs, while Facebook and Instagram Ads reach homeowners earlier and suit planned work such as EV chargers, panel upgrades and lighting. The right split depends on your job mix and capacity.

The short answer

If you only had budget for one channel, start where your best jobs come from. Urgent faults and ready-to-book requests lean towards Google Ads. Planned, higher-value installs lean towards Meta Ads (Facebook and Instagram). Most contractors eventually use both because they catch homeowners at different moments.

How Google Ads work for electricians

With Google Ads you appear when someone searches for something like a tripping circuit, a panel upgrade or an EV charger installer. The homeowner already has a need, so intent is high and the decision window is short. The trade-off is competition: in busy areas, clicks for electrician-related searches can be costly, and a slow reply wastes the spend. Campaign structure matters, because a search for a quick outlet repair and a search for a full panel upgrade are very different jobs.

How Facebook and Instagram Ads work for electricians

Meta Ads show your offer to homeowners in your area who are not searching yet. That makes them useful for work people plan rather than react to — adding an EV charger, upgrading an older panel, improving lighting. Good creative shows real local work, and a custom form asks about the job and timing so you are not drowning in casual enquiries. The trade-off is that interest is created rather than captured, so not everyone is ready to book straight away.

Which electrical jobs suit which channel?

  • Urgent repairs and faults: mostly Google Ads, where people search in the moment.
  • Panel upgrades: both. Google for those already searching; Meta for homeowners with older homes who have not yet acted. See our guide to panel upgrade leads.
  • EV chargers and lighting: often strong on Meta because the work is planned and visual.
  • Whole-home rewiring: a mix, with heavy emphasis on qualification and a booked estimate.

How to decide your split

Start from your numbers rather than a rule of thumb. Work out your average job value by type, how many extra jobs your crew can take, and what a booked estimate is worth. Then test: run both channels for a period, compare cost per booked estimate rather than cost per lead, and move budget to what produces booked work. This is illustrative advice, not a benchmark — your market will behave differently.

Whichever channel you choose

Both channels fail if replies are slow. A homeowner who submits a form usually contacts several electricians, so the first helpful reply tends to win.
