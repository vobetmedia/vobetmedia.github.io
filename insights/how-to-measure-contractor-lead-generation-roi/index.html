How to Measure Contractor Lead Generation ROI

Cost per lead is easy to see and easy to misread. This is how to measure what advertising really returns.

In short

To measure contractor lead generation ROI, track the full chain: ad spend, leads, booked estimates, jobs won and revenue or margin. Calculate cost per lead, per booked estimate and per job, and compare channels on cost per job and return on ad spend rather than on lead cost alone.

Start with the chain

ROI is only visible if you follow an enquiry all the way through: ad spend, leads, qualified leads, booked estimates, estimates won and revenue. If you only measure the first two, you can end up optimising towards cheap leads that never become work.

The metrics that matter

  • Cost per lead: spend ÷ leads.
  • Cost per booked estimate: spend ÷ booked estimates.
  • Close rate: jobs won ÷ estimates.
  • Cost per job: spend ÷ jobs won.
  • Return on ad spend: revenue from won jobs ÷ spend.
  • Margin on those jobs: what you actually keep.

A worked example

Illustrative numbers, not a client result: spend $2,000 in a month and receive 100 leads, so cost per lead is $20. 30 become booked estimates, so cost per booked estimate is about $67. You win 9 jobs, so cost per job is about $222 and close rate is 30%. If those jobs average a certain value and margin, you can compare that cost per job with the profit each job brings. The point is the method, not the figures.

Where verified examples fit

Our published landscaping case study shows the chain for one client: 52 leads at about $17 each on $877 of ad spend, 15 jobs closed and a 29% lead-to-close rate. See the roofing case study for 74 leads and 33 booked inspections. These are individual results, not benchmarks.

Get the tracking right

  • Keep ad accounts in your own name so data is yours.
  • Track forms and calls back to campaigns.
  • Record the source on every lead in your CRM and update outcomes.
  • Allow for lag: larger projects can take weeks or months to close.

Watch for common mistakes

Do not judge a campaign after a few days, do not compare channels on lead cost alone, and do not count booked appointments as revenue. Keep booked estimates and jobs won as separate numbers. Read more about our approach on the contractor lead generation page, or fix missed leads first.
